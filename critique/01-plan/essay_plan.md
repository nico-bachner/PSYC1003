# 1. Plan the essay

## Claim and proposed thesis

Video 1 says inability to remember **whether** something traumatic happened usually reflects protective blocking. It also allows that memories may not return and symptoms may improve regardless. Neither guaranteed accuracy nor specific suggestive techniques are stated.

Proposed thesis, still awaiting user review:

> Although documented trauma can go unreported and some delayed memories are corroborated, the reviewed evidence does not establish that protective blocking usually explains uncertainty about a possible traumatic event. Comparing nonreport, laboratory retrieval control and alternative explanations shows why the video's causal reassurance exceeds the evidence.

The central distinction is between evidence that an event occurred, evidence of a later gap, the cause of that gap, and accuracy of subsequent recollection. Keep the verdict bounded to the [six-source evidence record](../02-research/evidence_table.md).

## Paragraph plan

These allocations are planning targets, not assessment rules; total 920 words including citations and subheadings.

| Paragraph | Words | Argument and evidence |
|---|---:|---|
| Introduction | 120 | Relevance, precise Video 1 claim, thesis and route. |
| Supporting evidence | 210 | Williams: documented events and later nonreport. Geraerts (2007): some corroborated delayed recollections. Neither demonstrates earlier protective blocking. |
| Mechanism and alternatives | 230 | Pair Hulbert and Anderson with Catarino; retain the nonsignificant Experiment 2 caveat and population differences. Geraerts (2009) supplies possible alternative cognitive processes. |
| Critical synthesis | 130 | Give the strongest convergence argument a fair hearing, then explain why separate findings do not establish the causal sequence or the frequency implied by “usually.” |
| Audience implications | 130 | Credit reassurance and improvement without recovery; explain possible unwarranted certainty about an unknown event. Anderson measures beliefs, not video effects. Judge scientific communication without asserting measured net psychological harm. |
| Conclusion | 100 | Summarise the qualified verdict and distinguish empathy from confirming a hidden event; no new evidence. |
