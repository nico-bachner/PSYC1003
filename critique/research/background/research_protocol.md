## Question and scope

Does published scientific evidence justify the statement that inability to remember a possible traumatic event is **usually** caused by protective blocking of access to its memory?

Assess three separate propositions:

1. **Memory gaps:** whether people fail to report or recall independently documented events, or report having forgotten them.
2. **Mechanism:** whether the evidence identifies protective retrieval blocking, distinguishing it from encoding failures, ordinary forgetting, unwillingness to disclose, developmental forgetting, cue dependence and reinterpretation.
3. **Accuracy:** whether later recollections are corroborated or vulnerable to distortion. Accurate later recall does not by itself establish the mechanism of the earlier gap; experimentally induced false memories do not establish that all recovered memories are false.

The word *usually* requires evidence about frequency and causes in the relevant population. A selected clinical sample, a correlation with dissociation, or a laboratory demonstration of retrieval suppression cannot alone supply that population estimate.

Treat the speaker fairly: the transcript allows memories not to return and symptoms to improve regardless. It does not explicitly prescribe suggestive recovery methods or guarantee recovered-memory accuracy. Do not evaluate all trauma treatments or EMDR efficacy.

## Selection criteria

Prioritise published scientific papers that add a distinct test or necessary context: documented-event follow-up, retrieval suppression, alternative explanations of delayed recall, corroboration, autobiographical memory distortion, and public beliefs. Include strong support even when it does not establish the full claim. Reviews can frame disputes and lead to primary studies but must be labelled as reviews.

For each retained paper record complete citation and DOI/URL; publication and review status; access and verification level; study design, sample, measures and comparisons; source findings and page/table/section locators; author limitations and separate appraisal; precise relevance; and tentative essay role. Record any uncertain metadata rather than inventing it.

Exclude an unreviewed preprint from the published-science shortlist unless a published version is verified. In particular, check the current status of the supplied ter Beek et al. manuscript (course/week-06/6-1.pdf).
