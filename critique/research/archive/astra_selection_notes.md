## Assessment of the partial reviewed set

- **Williams (1994): strong provisional inclusion.** A documented-event cohort directly addresses whether subsequent nonreport can occur. It does not directly measure inaccessible memory or explain nonreport. The older-child subgroup helps prevent dismissing all findings as infantile amnesia. Both the cohort selection and the 17-year delay limit generalisation to uncertain viewers.
- **Williams (1995): useful reserve or paired use.** Adds delayed-recall accuracy information, but uses the same cohort as the 1994 paper and cannot count as an independent replication. Prefer a distinct corroboration study if the reference/word budget is tight.
- **Geraerts et al. (2007): strong provisional inclusion.** Helps treat later accuracy fairly, including the possibility of corroborated spontaneous recovery. The observational recovery-context comparison does not establish therapy causation, and zero corroboration in 16 cases is not proof that every report was false.
- **Geraerts et al. (2009): provisional inclusion.** Adds potential explanations for the *experience* of recovery, but retrospective groups and laboratory tasks do not establish the cause of any participant's autobiographical memory gap. Keep its DRM word-list findings distinct from autobiographical false-memory implantation.
- **Catarino et al. (2015): provisional mechanism countercheck.** Suppression is measurable, but reduced suppression in PTSD complicates a general claim of trauma-driven protective blocking. It requires an affirmative suppression study alongside it if used to represent that experimental literature.
- **Loftus and Pickrell (1995): reserve.** The deliberately suggested false-event experiment contributes a distinct accuracy caution, but this is a small non-traumatic paradigm and the speaker does not explicitly advocate suggestion. It could distract from the causal claim if given a full paragraph. Sol checked a near-final manuscript, not the precise final typeset version; retain that access caveat if selected.
