# Essay Instructions

A 750-1000 word essay must be submitted on Canvas by 11:59 pm AEST on Friday of Week 9. The essay will be worth 25% of the total course mark.

**Background:** Misinformation is a serious problem across social media platforms. Scientific data are frequently misinterpreted, oversimplified, or presented in a biased manner to suit a particular agenda.

**This assessment asks you to identify and analyse the science behind a specific claim made by a media personality with no formal scientific training. You will be given 3 examples to choose from; the files will be made available on this page in Week 5.**

**Select ONE of the videos to discuss. Identify the main argument (i.e., the point the speaker is trying to make). Search the scientific literature to find evidence for and against the argument. Based on the evidence you find, decide whether you agree or disagree.**

---

## Structure

### 1) Introduction (~1 paragraph)

- Briefly introduce the relevant area of research. Indicate why the field is interesting/relevant (i.e., why would most people care about the topic?)
- Introduce the video and summarise the argument that the speaker is attempting to make.
- Provide a thesis statement that **a)** tells us whether or not you believe that the speaker's argument is ultimately supported by scientific evidence, and **b)** outlines how your essay will demonstrate this.

### 2) Body*

- **Affirmative:** Discuss scientific evidence supporting the presenter's argument.
- **Negative:** Discuss scientific evidence contradicting the presenter's argument.
- **Critical Analysis:** Explain why you think the scientific evidence that you discussed ultimately supports or refutes the argument made by the presenter (or whether it's a little bit of both). Discuss any impacts that you think the video might have if viewed by an audience with no scientific background. Do you think its impact is helpful or harmful, on balance?

\*You may use these 3 bullet points as section headings, but it is not mandatory.

### 3) Conclusion (1 paragraph)

Provide a brief summary of the main points of your essay.

### 4) Reference List (not included in word count)

---

## Things to consider

- This is an assessment with a fair amount of flexibility. There are multiple arguments that you can make for each video, and you may choose to argue either side. Your mark will NOT be impacted by whether or not you agree with the speaker.
- The "Critical Analysis" component of the essay gives you a chance to provide your thoughts on questions like: *How effectively or accurately did the speaker interpret the scientific evidence? Is the science being distorted or misrepresented to fit an agenda? What harm/benefit, if any, might come from this type of messaging?* HOWEVER, keep in mind that the writing style should be professional and scientific. Avoid using phrases like "I think..." or "I feel...". Make sure your argument is objective and evidence based.
- You need to select appropriate literature to cite in the body of your essay (i.e., websites, blogs, etc. are **not** considered appropriate sources; published scientific literature is appropriate). You should use empirical evidence (e.g., published journal articles) to support your evaluation of the speaker's claims. The number of citations needed will depend on your argument, but an essay of this length can be effectively constructed using ~5-10 references.
- You may address the same research in your "affirmative" and "negative" sections, if you choose. For instance, one study may partially support the speaker's claim (affirmative), but perhaps certain details of the research weaken the strength of their argument (negative). Alternatively, you may address separate studies in each section.

## Formatting

- You should include a title that you form yourself (i.e., do not repeat the essay topic word-for-word). Near the title indicate (by number) which video you will be addressing.
- Include a cover page with the title, your student ID, and the course title (you may also include your tutor's name). The cover page is NOT included in word count.
- The 750-1000 word limit is strict. There will be a 2.5% penalty for a word count of 1001-1100, plus a further 2.5% penalty for every 50 words thereafter.
- In-text citations and subheadings (if used) are included in the word count.
- The reference list itself is not included in the word count.
- Do not include an abstract or appendices in your essay.
- Please use Arial or Times New Roman font size 12 and double-spacing.
- Referencing (both in-text citations and the reference list) should be completed using APA style (as per the APA manual’s 7th edition; see: [APA Formatting](https://www.library.sydney.edu.au/support/referencing/apa)).
